## Origins

I spent 21 years in the United States Army — enlisted Signal Corps 
as a network operator and analyst, then commissioned as an Infantry 
officer and later Civil Affairs. After college in Indiana and a short 
career as an Assembly Language and COBOL programmer at an insurance company in 
Michigan, I joined the Army in 1999. After retirement as a Major, 
I earned a masters in cybersecurity, worked a year for the FBI, 
then spent several years protecting critical technical defense programs 
at the Department of Defense. In September 2026 I transitioned to the 
Department of Justice.

The investment thesis didn't start with a research report. It started 
with a hobby. As a college student back in Indiana, I used to calculate 
when and where Iridium satellites would be visible in the night sky — 
tracking the constellation pass by pass. Years later I realized I could 
own a piece of it. That's when the picks-and-shovels idea took hold.

If the people building the next generation of weapons systems need 
titanium alloys, advanced composites, hypersonic test platforms, and 
AI command software — why not own the companies supplying those things? 
Not the primes everyone knows. The layer beneath them.

This screener is my attempt to systematize that instinct.

## The Philosophy

The universe is the roster. The active portfolio is the starting 
lineup. The screener is the coaching staff's analytics system.

Players rotate based on performance. The best players are always 
on the field. When form drops, they go to the bench. When a new 
player earns their spot, they come off the bench.

The goal is never to find the one perfect stock. The goal is to 
continuously keep the best companies on the field.

In the Army we say you can always improve your fighting position. 
There is never nothing to do. That ethos drives this project — 
continuous improvement, no finish line.

## The Build

The project started with a simple question: what if I could apply 
the same discipline I used in the Army — clear objectives, documented 
processes, measurable outcomes — to investing?

The answer was a rules-based screener built from scratch. Not a 
copy of someone else's system. Not a black box. Every decision 
documented, every signal explainable, every limitation honestly stated.

We started with the thesis. Then the universe. Then the code. 
In that order. Always in that order.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (June 2026)
- Defined the investment thesis: picks-and-shovels play on the 
  hypersonic and next-generation defense supply chain
- Built a 12-ticker universe across four technology domains
- Wrote a rules-based screener with technical, fundamental, 
  and thesis scoring
- Backtested against 5 years of historical data
- Launched the Second Layer Capital brand: X @SecondLayerCap, 
  LinkedIn, logo

### Phase 2 — Intelligence (June–July 2026)
- Added automated monitoring of Pentagon contracts, 
  Congressional defense legislation, and SEC IPO filings
- Built a discovery scanner to continuously surface 
  new universe candidates from defense ETFs and SEC filings
- Deployed a live dashboard — fought Streamlit Cloud rate 
  limiting, Schwab OAuth token expiration, and yfinance 
  instability before settling on a GitHub Actions daily 
  briefing as the reliable mobile solution
- Initiated live trading in Robinhood Agentic account June 16

### Phase 3 — Execution (August–September 2026)
- Built portfolio tracker, rebalancing engine with 30-day 
  drift buffer, signal history tracker, drift history tracker
- Added earnings calendar integration and social media 
  post generator
- Automated rebalancing execution with manual confirmation
- Expanded portfolio from 4 to 9 active positions

## Key Decisions Along the Way

**The Dashboard Saga**
We tried Streamlit Cloud. It broke. We fixed it. It broke again. 
Switched from yfinance to Tradier. Tradier's token expired. 
Switched to Schwab. Schwab's OAuth token expired on the cloud. 
Eventually settled on GitHub Actions generating a daily markdown 
briefing readable on iPhone via GitHub mobile. Sometimes the 
simple solution is the right one.

**PLTR — The Special Case**
Palantir doesn't fit the strict picks-and-shovels definition — 
it's not a supplier, it's a software platform. But there's no 
public proxy for what it does at the DoD AI command layer. 
Gotham and AIP are the operating system for the modern data-driven 
military. No smaller company does what PLTR does. Special case 
exception granted. Q2 2026 earnings validated the thesis — 
revenue up 93% YoY, U.S. commercial up 149%. The stock surged 
29% in a single day.

**MTRN — The Reclassification**
Materion started as an Anchor — a defensive, diversified materials 
supplier. Then Q2 2026 happened. Record earnings beat, 42.2% 
revenue growth YoY, all-time high backlog including a $15M 
commercial space engine contract. Reclassified to Standard. 
The screener adapts to the business, not the other way around.

**KRMN — The Governance Watch**
Karman Holdings has the strongest hypersonics thesis in the 
universe — payload fairings, interstage structures, purpose-built 
for the next generation of missiles and space launch vehicles. 
Then the CFO departed unexpectedly in August 2026 and a short 
seller attacked the acquisition governance. The business is fine. 
The stock is not. Thesis intact, position
